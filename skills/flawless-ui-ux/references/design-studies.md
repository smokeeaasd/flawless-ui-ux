# Design studies

Use a relevant study when choosing a new direction or resolving a composition problem. These original, fictional examples demonstrate decisions across a whole system. They are reference material, not templates or production interfaces. Inspect the linked image when the host can display it; the written anatomy remains usable without image tools. Load only the studies that inform the current decision.

## Find a useful principle

| Decision to resolve | Study | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| Lead with a known offer or explain a service first | [Service offer](#service-offer) | Two structures for the same facts, with different reading paths |
| Keep repeated decisions fast and understandable | [Operational workspace](#operational-workspace) | Dense alignment, functional emphasis, and local actions |
| Help a reader understand and return to a topic | [Editorial guide](#editorial-guide) | Type roles, reading measure, supporting navigation, and examples |
| Let visual work lead without losing orientation | [Visual collection](#visual-collection) | Image scale, sequence, metadata, and a detail/return path |

A study is useful because of the relationship it solves, not its industry or style label. A quiet screen can be highly specific; an expressive screen can remain clear. The collection is deliberately small, not a complete vocabulary of styles. Existing brand material or a supplied reference may be a better basis.

The families visible in these plates illustrate type roles, not a preferred font list. Choose the actual project's families using [Typography](craft.md#typography); do not copy a study's Arial or Georgia merely because it appears in the reference.

## Service offer

[View the service study](../assets/design-studies/service.png).

**Situation:** a rider needs repair prices and arrival instructions. Both proposals use exactly the same workshop facts. Their difference is the question answered first.

**Price-led anatomy:** workshop name and navigation; a direct service heading; three aligned service/price rows; a visit action; hours, address, and the confirmed repair process. The offer earns the largest share of the opening. Dark green ink, warm paper, and a yellow action have separate roles. The compact sans headings and tabular prices suit quick comparison.

**Process-led anatomy:** workshop name and navigation; a short explanation of arrival, assessment, and collection; a visit action; prices and practical details immediately afterward. A strong heading and a vertical process line organize the opening instead of three equal feature cards. It serves visitors who need to understand what happens before they commit to a visit.

**Carry the system:** reuse the alignment and price treatment in service details. Give location and hours their own readable group. If a contact form exists, its field labels and feedback use the reading typography, while the page's action color stays reserved for useful actions.

The visit action uses a direct label without an appended arrow. Headings explain different parts of the offer rather than repeating the same benefit as several slogans. Transfer that economy of expression, not a fixed number of headings or icons.

**Adaptation and states:** on a phone, service names and prices remain paired, and the arrival action follows the relevant information. Navigation and focus remain visible. A visit link leads to actual visit information; opening hours do not justify claiming the workshop is currently open without the necessary time context.

**Quality target:** a reader can compare the three prices and find arrival instructions without deciphering promotional wording. Choose the first structure when price comparison leads the decision, the second when uncertainty about the process leads it. The same facts must survive either choice. Transfer the hierarchy and action placement, not the fictional name, colors, or repair theme.

## Operational workspace

[View the workspace study](../assets/design-studies/workspace.png).

**Situation:** a receptionist finds a bicycle, understands its repair status, and marks completed work ready for collection.

**Anatomy:** a compact navigation column; queue heading and scoped count; search and status filters; aligned rows with identifier, customer, work, due date, status, and amount; a selected-record panel with the next permitted action. A repeated baseline and tabular amounts make comparison easier than individually decorated cards. Neutral surfaces support sustained scanning; indigo marks selection and actions, while an amber status includes explicit wording.

**Carry the system:** details, edit fields, confirmation, and empty results reuse the same type roles and spacing. Selection, keyboard focus, and repair status remain distinguishable. A row's hover treatment does not replace the selected state. The selected panel names the record so an action cannot be mistaken for a queue-wide command.

**Adaptation and states:** a narrow layout turns each row into a labeled record with the same key information; detail opens on demand with a return path preserving search and filters. In a ready-only view, marking a record ready may change membership and counts according to the actual query. Empty results retain filters and a clear-reset action; a failed update retains the record and offers recovery.

**Quality target:** the identifier, status, and next action remain easy to locate in a full queue and a filtered result. Useful density is achieved through grouping and alignment, not tiny type. Transfer that operational structure; do not dress every dashboard as a repair ledger.

## Editorial guide

[View the reading study](../assets/design-studies/reading.png).

**Situation:** a reader learns how to care for wooden furniture, jumps to a specific topic, and returns to the explanation.

**Anatomy:** a publication heading, a prominent serif title, a short introduction, a quiet contents rail, and a bounded reading column with examples. Serif display type establishes the editorial character; simple sans labels support navigation. Warm white, dark ink, and a restrained rust accent keep paragraphs visually stable. Section spacing and meaningful headings carry the rhythm.

**Carry the system:** figures have captions near the material they explain; notes are visually secondary but readable. Related reading follows the article. A warning's prominence comes from its consequence and wording, not from treating every note as an alert.

**Adaptation and states:** move contents navigation above the article when the rail stops fitting. Keep the anchor destination clear of sticky navigation, preserve readable measure at large widths, and let user-enlarged text reflow. Links describe their destinations. Code or tables, if relevant to another subject, get their own readable treatment.

**Quality target:** a reader can distinguish title, section, paragraph, note, and navigation at a glance and resume reading after a jump. Transfer the division between reading and wayfinding; serif type and paper colors are one possible expression, not a requirement for articles.

## Visual collection

[View the collection study](../assets/design-studies/collection.png).

**Situation:** a visitor explores a fictional collection of graphic works and opens an item for a closer look. The artworks in the study are original geometric specimens, not placeholders for unavailable client photography.

**Anatomy:** a compact identity and navigation; a leading work with room to be seen; a varied but aligned sequence; concise title, category, and year adjacent to each work. Warm yellow grounds the page; dark ink anchors navigation; the artworks supply the strongest color differences. The type stays restrained so the work carries the visual character.

**Carry the system:** detail retains the work's title and context, gives imagery appropriate scale, and exposes next/previous and return actions. Crops are chosen per work rather than forcing every image into an arbitrary shape. Additional information follows the artwork without becoming another marketing section.

**Adaptation and states:** narrow layouts preserve deliberate order and readable metadata. Selection works by keyboard and touch as well as pointer. Returning from detail restores the collection's position and active filters where applicable. If an image fails, its title and destination remain understandable; reduced motion preserves all navigation.

**Quality target:** the work dominates without hiding how to explore it. Transfer the relationship among artifact, metadata, and navigation. An image-led direction needs suitable actual material; when that is missing, choose a viable content-led structure or acquire assets within scope.

## Use the studies in a real project

Choose a useful principle and translate it into the current content: what leads, how supporting information relates, which type and color roles persist, what actions do, and how the layout changes. Keep this as a short working target in the conversation or existing project record. Do not place the design rationale into visitor-facing copy.

When alternatives would change the visitor's experience, compare their structure and tradeoff, not merely palette swaps. With a clear brief, choose and implement; present a choice when the user asks or when a consequential ambiguity needs their decision. An existing coherent identity supplies the visual system for additions and focused refinements.

A supplied screenshot, sketch, or suitable generated mockup can make the target more concrete. Inspect it before using it. Use image generation only when available, relevant, and authorized; it is not a required step. Without it, work directly in code from the structural target. A pictured interface still needs accessible text, responsive composition, functioning states, and real content.

At review, compare the implemented hierarchy, proportions, type roles, assets, and states with the chosen target at representative sizes. Explain material departures through product needs, access, content, or technical constraints. Correct unexplained drift within scope. Use [Review](review.md) to separate visual observations from behavior tests and unverified expectations.
