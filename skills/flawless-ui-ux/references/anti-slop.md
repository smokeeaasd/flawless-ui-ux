# Contextual prevention

Use the final pass before delivering creation or redesign, and the relevant checks for focused work. Diagnose the pattern's effect; appearance alone does not establish authorship or poor quality.

## Final pass

Inspect the implemented target as a whole, including lower sections and working states. Use source for copy and icon use; use rendered inspection for visual relationships when available.

- **Arrows and icons:** inspect buttons, links, captions, cards, and illustrations together. Remove arrows that merely decorate an already clear action. Retain useful direction, disclosure, chart meaning, and recognizable interaction cues; preserve or replace a sole action affordance before removing it.
- **Copy:** read headings, descriptions, notes, and footer in sequence. Group repeated promises by meaning, including rephrased reassurance and rhetorical contrasts. Replace redundant wording with the distinct confirmed information needed there, or remove it. Preserve deliberate brand voice and protected text.
- **Composition and treatment:** check repeated region shapes, competing emphasis, type roles, default styling, decoration, and unsupported proof. Use the relevant sections below when a signal needs diagnosis; retain choices that serve the product.

Correct authorized issues, then recheck the affected content, controls, and layout. Preserve identity, useful density, and visual richness. During review-only work or outside the edit scope, report material findings instead. Keep unobserved visual effects explicit; a source pass cannot establish them.

## Composition and substance

Check whether hierarchy and region structure come from the content or an interchangeable template. Similar cards can help comparison; they are less useful when unrelated information receives identical emphasis. Develop different proportions or structures where the material needs them.

A rich opening needs useful continuation. Lower regions should explain, compare, demonstrate, or support action rather than repeat the opening's promise. Keep useful content and intentional visual presence during cleanup.

## Visual language and system fit

Look for decorative elements competing with the work, incompatible treatments, or a palette swap over an unsuitable composition. A familiar convention can be the best solution when executed deliberately.

Correct the specific relationship through scale, alignment, type, imagery, grouping, or color distribution. Cards, gradients, shadows, large type, dark surfaces, minimalism, and vivid colors are contextual choices rather than banned styles.

## Typography and emphasis

Inspect actual type roles, readability, loaded fonts, and the balance between display and working content. Scaffold defaults deserve assessment; unfamiliar fonts do not automatically improve identity. Keep variation meaningful instead of making every heading, caption, and number equally emphatic.

Small, faint operational content beside oversized summaries can undermine the task. Increase its usable presence or rebalance the composition without flattening all roles.

## Copy and proof

Read the actual copy as a sequence. Repeated reassurance, rhetorical contrasts, metaphors, and payoffs can make operational content feel promotional and leave sections without distinct information. Use the concrete explanation or label needed there. Preserve expressive language when its role is deliberate and different.

Check facts, terms, sample labels, and destinations. Remove invented customer proof, unsupported metrics, awards, and testimonials rather than replacing them with different unverified claims. Copy cleanup preserves the visual design unless wider changes are authorized.

## Imagery and motion

Check whether imagery shows relevant material or merely decorates a product-shaped slot. A conceptual illustration can be appropriate; a misleading representation of an actual customer, team, or product is a different issue. Preserve honest imagery and improve its relationship to the content.

Use motion to clarify change or support the experience. Repeated entrances, delayed content, or scroll effects that impede reading need correction. Keep a useful reduced-motion experience.

## Affordance and task flow

Investigate overlays, excessive icon-only actions, redundant arrows, and navigation that promises a destination but only moves within unrelated content. Choose presentation for the task's duration and context; a dialog is useful for a bounded action and less useful for sustained work.

Keep functioning behavior when changing presentation. An unnecessary feature can be removed within scope; an explicitly requested interaction must remain. Check relevant widths and states for clipped labels, unreachable controls, and broken transitions. Judge the observed effect rather than seeking a zero-pattern count.

## Worked diagnoses

**Three identical cards for different jobs.** A service page places menu, hours and address in equally prominent cards with the same icon, title and sentence. The issue is not the card shape itself: visitors must extract a price, a schedule and a location from three different information types. A menu list, aligned hours and a concise address/visit region better expose those jobs. Keep the same facts and character. If the cards instead compare three subscription plans with the same fields, their repetition can support the task.

**A polished dashboard hides the work.** Four oversized summary tiles and a large decorative chart push records below the fold. Find which summary actually changes a decision. Reduce or relocate supporting totals, align the records, attach states and actions to their object, and keep relevant numeric comparisons. Do not remove all summaries or make the table tiny to obtain a fashionable sparse layout. The question is whether the operational task leads.

**A public page repeats its payoff.** The opening promises faster scheduling; a benefit repeats time savings; the next section says “less admin, more time”; the footer repeats the same reassurance. Replace only authorized copy with distinct confirmed information: how scheduling works, who can edit, the actual limitation, or the next action. If no additional facts exist, a shorter page can be more honest. Do not invent proof to fill the removed space.

**A refinement substitutes a stereotype.** A vivid collection is made “professional” by removing color, imagery and scale, leaving anonymous bordered cards. Even if spacing is neater, the requested identity may have been lost. Diagnose readability, navigation or balance directly and keep expression where it serves the work. Neutrality, unfamiliar type and reduced decoration are not universal signs of quality.

**A demonstration promises production behavior.** A local form displays “Booking confirmed” without a service or persistence. This is a product truth failure, not a tone preference. Use a truthful local result and retain entered data; make the integration boundary visible near the action. Do not manufacture network delay or a success count to make the example feel real.

Apply these as reasoning examples, not a blacklist of shapes or words. If protected copy or structure contains an issue, report it without silently widening the task. A useful final pass improves the actual product while preserving its valid decisions.
