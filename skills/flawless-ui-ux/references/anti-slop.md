# Contextual prevention

Use when a recurring pattern creates a concrete design or content problem. Diagnose its effect; appearance alone does not establish authorship or poor quality.

## Composition and substance

Check whether hierarchy and region structure come from the content or an interchangeable template. Similar cards can help comparison; they are less useful when unrelated information receives identical emphasis. Develop different proportions or structures where the material needs them.

A rich opening needs useful continuation. Lower regions should explain, compare, demonstrate, or support action rather than repeat the opening's promise. Keep useful content and intentional visual presence during cleanup.

## Visual language and system fit

Look for decorative elements competing with the work, incompatible treatments, or a palette swap over an unsuitable composition. A familiar convention can be the best solution when executed deliberately.

Correct the specific relationship through scale, alignment, type, imagery, grouping, or color distribution. Cards, gradients, shadows, large type, dark surfaces, minimalism, and vivid colors are contextual choices rather than banned styles.

## Typography and emphasis

Inspect actual type roles, readability, loaded fonts, and the balance between display and working content. Scaffold defaults deserve assessment; unfamiliar fonts do not automatically improve identity. Keep variation meaningful instead of making every heading, caption, and number equally emphatic.

Small, faint operational content beside oversized summaries can undermine the task. Increase its usable presence or rebalance the composition without flattening all roles.

## Copy and proof

Read the actual copy as a sequence. Repeated reassurance, rhetorical contrasts, metaphors, and payoffs can make operational content feel promotional and leave sections without distinct information. Use the concrete explanation or label needed there. Preserve expressive language when its role is deliberate and different.

Check facts, terms, sample labels, and destinations. Remove invented customer proof, unsupported metrics, awards, and testimonials rather than replacing them with different unverified claims. Copy cleanup preserves the visual design unless wider changes are authorized.

## Imagery and motion

Check whether imagery shows relevant material or merely decorates a product-shaped slot. A conceptual illustration can be appropriate; a misleading representation of an actual customer, team, or product is a different issue. Preserve honest imagery and improve its relationship to the content.

Use motion to clarify change or support the experience. Repeated entrances, delayed content, or scroll effects that impede reading need correction. Keep a useful reduced-motion experience.

## Affordance and task flow

Investigate overlays, excessive icon-only actions, redundant arrows, and navigation that promises a destination but only moves within unrelated content. Choose presentation for the task's duration and context; a dialog is useful for a bounded action and less useful for sustained work.

Keep functioning behavior when changing presentation. An unnecessary feature can be removed within scope; an explicitly requested interaction must remain. Check relevant widths and states for clipped labels, unreachable controls, and broken transitions. Judge the observed effect rather than seeking a zero-pattern count.
