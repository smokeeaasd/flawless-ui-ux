# AI slop prevention

Use when establishing a direction, replacing an incumbent treatment, or investigating generic-looking output. During refinement and review, read only the sections relevant to the authorized target. Treat the signals below as prompts for a decision: a familiar visual device alone does not establish poor quality or AI authorship.

## Apply within the intervention

Before implementing a new direction, connect its major choices to the product, audience, content, or task. After implementation, inspect whether those choices actually work. Keep an explicit brand decision unless replacing it is authorized; correct its readability or behavior within the allowed scope.

For a suspected pattern, establish:

1. The visible signal and the region or state affected.
2. Its role in this product and any explicit constraint that protects it.
3. The consequence for comprehension, use, credibility, or coherence.
4. A decision to retain, adapt, or replace it, followed by an appropriate check.

These are working judgments, not a required report or a numerical score. Distinguish a demonstrated usability failure from a stylistic suggestion. Reducing decoration can improve focus, but removing substance can produce an equally generic empty page.

## Composition and substance

**Signals:** a centered opening followed by interchangeable feature blocks regardless of the brief; identical emphasis for unrelated information; a large number displacing the actual task; containers nested around the same content; equal spacing that hides grouping; a polished hero above unfinished lower sections.

Ask what each region lets the visitor understand or do. A repair page needs scope, starting prices, process, and a useful assessment path. A queue needs recognizable records, states, and actions. Replacing either with generic summaries leaves the real work unexplained.

Organize from those relationships. Give different information different roles, establish alignment and grouping, and remove a container when it adds no ownership, manipulation, or useful boundary. Keep real content and working actions as decorative layers are reduced. Do not replace every card with an enormous gap or a slogan.

**Legitimate use:** comparable subscription plans can use repeated panels; an ordered process can have numbered steps; an operations screen can be intentionally dense. Preserve the useful relationship and improve hierarchy where needed. Minimalism is about relevance to the task rather than a required flat visual style. [Usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/).

**Check:** follow the actual information or action path through the full target. Compare related and unrelated spacing, content completeness, and the prominence of the real task at affected widths.

## Visual language and system fit

**Signals:** an accent on every region; decorative halos or patterned backgrounds that overwhelm the subject; blur, outlines, shadows, and large curves stacked on each panel; a badge that looks like a control without being one; unrelated colors or radii introduced into an established system. An automatic beige treatment can be as interchangeable as an automatic saturated one.

Assign each treatment a job. Use emphasis for a meaningful priority, a boundary for an actual region, and depth for a useful relationship. Start from existing tokens and components. If a local exception is justified, make its relationship to the system clear instead of silently creating another scale.

An intentional gradient, expressive palette, glass surface, or soft radius can serve a brand or explain layering. Preserve it when it does. Adjust competing emphasis, contrast, layering, or spacing before proposing an identity replacement. A pill may be appropriate for a real status or selectable filter; its appearance should agree with its role.

**Check:** identify what color, shape, and depth communicate with real content in place. Check relevant states, neighboring components, and actual rendered contrast. A color value or effect name in source does not establish its visual impact by itself.

## Typography and emphasis

**Signals:** an oversized headline crowds out the explanation and action; tiny supporting text compensates for it; a decorative caption repeats its heading; default type treatment ignores product character; display italics, monospace, uppercase, or extreme tracking are applied without a reading role. Awkward wraps can also interrupt otherwise sound hierarchy.

Establish the roles first: title, section heading, running text, label, note, and comparable value. Fit size, weight, measure, leading, and spacing to those roles. Repair an orphaned last word or a clipped label using authorized changes rather than forcing one viewport's line breaks into every size.

One family can support a complete hierarchy. Familiar system fonts can suit a product or a constrained local build. Monospace can help identifiers or code, and expressive type can belong to a real identity. Preserve those uses while correcting indistinct roles or uncomfortable reading. Changing fonts solely to avoid recognition is not a meaningful direction.

**Check:** inspect actual fonts and weights, real copy and long labels, wide and narrow wrapping, and text emphasis relative to the task. Use [Craft](craft.md#typography) for execution details; preserve fixed wording and layout during a typography-only request.

## Copy and product truth

**Signals:** interchangeable promises without an explanation; repeated slogan contrasts; the same field label echoed in headings and help text; punctuation or capitalization used as a substitute for information; invented counters, customers, quotations, credentials, or outcomes. A working-looking control can also falsely imply an unavailable integration.

Name what the visitor can do, what they receive, and the confirmed constraint or consequence. For a restoration service, an agreed written estimate explains more than an unsupported claim of exceptional innovation. Remove repeated text when it answers no additional question, while retaining useful instructions and field-specific help.

Expressive copy can support the product's voice. Preserve it when the subject, offer, and next action remain understandable. A verified business figure may be important; an illustrative number must be identified as sample content. Neither novelty nor an attractive arrangement makes unsupported proof acceptable.

**Check:** trace material claims to supplied facts and controls to actual outcomes. Inspect error, pending, and completion wording too. A demo must describe the local result without claiming a request, payment, email, or persisted update occurred.

## Imagery and representation

**Signals:** generic shapes standing in for an unexplained product; invented interface or chart imagery implying a functioning feature; an unrelated stock scene presented as evidence; a heavy overlay that conceals the image's contribution; stretched, missing, or unfinished assets.

Choose what the image contributes: actual output, a process, place, object, or distinctive character. Use an honest source and an intentional crop. If no suitable asset exists, adapt the composition around useful content or a faithful illustration instead of fabricating proof. Simple SVG can communicate a real relationship; arbitrary detail and ornament do not make a placeholder informative.

Atmosphere imagery can establish a mood without representing the actual premises or product. Label previews or samples when their status matters. Keep essential information accessible as text and retain required credits.

**Check:** confirm the intended asset loads and that the focal point, crop, text relationship, and meaning survive the relevant sizes. Inspect full content rather than assuming a filename or source URL proves suitability.

## Affordance and task flow

**Signals:** decorative elements that resemble actions; icon containers more prominent than the feature; duplicate instructions around a simple field; essential actions available only on hover; an entire workflow compressed into an overlay that obscures information needed for the decision.

Make the visible form and name agree with the element's behavior. Use links for destinations, buttons for actions, and text for static information. Keep object actions and relevant consequences close to the object. Remove a decorative wrapper or icon when it adds no meaning; preserve useful recognition and status cues.

A focused dialog can be appropriate. When the task requires extensive browsing or comparing background information, consider an in-page region or route within the authorized scope. For a modal that remains, preserve its keyboard sequence, appropriate initial focus, and a sensible return location. [Modal dialog guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/).

**Check:** exercise the actual primary path and relevant recovery with the permitted input methods. Identify decorative previews honestly. A real control remains understandable and operable after visual cleanup.

## Attention and motion

**Signals:** idle status that continually pulses; static marketing copy made to look editable; automatically moving text competing with reading; every region entering with the same spectacle; an exaggerated transition that delays an ordinary action.

Connect motion to an actual change, relationship, feedback, or intentional expression. A pending action can indicate real activity; idle availability usually needs clear status rather than repeated attention. Keep text selectable, controls reachable, and essential content available without waiting for a reveal.

Expressive motion can belong to an experience, while a working screen often benefits from more restrained feedback. Respect reduced-motion preferences and applicable controls for ongoing movement. Automatically moving information lasting more than five seconds alongside other content needs a way to pause, stop, or hide it unless the movement is essential. Interaction-triggered nonessential motion has separate guidance. [Pause, Stop, Hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), [Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).

**Check:** observe movement during the relevant interaction or reading path and with reduced motion when possible. Static CSS identifies a risk, not the complete experienced result. Do not add animation merely to meet an imagined quota or remove useful pending feedback to make the page still.

## Responsive and resilient delivery

**Signals:** desktop composition only shrunk to a phone; meaningful content hidden to suppress overflow; text or actions covered by sticky layers; menus clipped by ancestors; content left invisible after an entrance effect fails; an attractive static frame with broken controls.

Adapt reading order, disclosure, navigation, and control placement to the task. Preserve the content and relationships that matter. A data table can need local scrolling; surrounding prose and actions still need a usable layout. Keep semantic information relationships aligned with the visual structure. [Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/info-and-relationships.html).

Resolve runtime errors and missing resources affecting the task before judging decorative finish. Prefer a visible baseline for content already available to the page rather than leaving it dependent on a reveal effect. Inspect open menus, long messages, dense content, and relevant keyboard or viewport constraints, not only a pristine first screen.

**Check:** use the actual task paths and states at representative sizes. Record which layouts and interactions were observed. Source inspection, screenshots, and a passing build establish different things.

## Verify and stop

Use [Review](review.md) for evidence and priorities. A contextual pattern becomes a finding when its consequence is established; otherwise identify the suggestion or uncertainty. Do not label a page AI-generated based on appearance. A read-only review leaves project files unchanged, and a focused intervention does not authorize unrelated cleanup.

After corrections, recheck factual content, working actions, protected brand decisions, hierarchy, and affected layouts. The result should retain useful substance and a coherent direction. Stop when material in-scope problems are resolved or a specific limitation prevents progress; do not keep restyling to eliminate every familiar device.
