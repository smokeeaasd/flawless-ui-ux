# Content interfaces

Use the purpose section that matches the visitor's task: persuasion, reading, or exploration. These sections specialize the selected creation, redesign, refinement, or review procedure. They do not require every page to contain a hero, pricing, a form, or a collection.

## Establish the material

Inspect the actual text, product facts, navigation, content model, brand decisions, and available media. Identify the intended next action or information need. Recover missing context with [Context](../context.md) rather than filling it with interchangeable claims.

Check what imagery can honestly demonstrate or establish. A location photograph, product screenshot, illustration, or typography-led treatment can be appropriate when it serves the subject. Do not imply that atmosphere images depict the actual product or team. Identify sample content and preserve required source credits. Fit the direction to available or permitted resources.

## Persuasion

Help the visitor understand the offer, judge whether it fits, and take an informed next action. Begin with the questions particular to this product: what it does, who it serves, what the visitor receives, relevant terms or limitations, evidence available, and how to proceed. Use only questions that the page actually needs to answer.

Map supplied content to those questions before arranging sections. Connect claims to explanations, demonstrations, or real proof. If proof is unavailable, improve the explanation instead of inventing customer logos, testimonials, performance figures, or awards. Use the actual product or output when it can demonstrate the offer.

Establish an opening that makes the subject and useful next action understandable. Let the content determine whether comparison, process, examples, pricing, or another structure should lead. Keep secondary actions available without making every region compete equally. Required prices, units, billing periods, hours, locations, or restrictions must stay accurate and easy to find.

Use [Direction](../direction.md) to resolve visual choices at the depth the brief needs. Keep type, composition, color, and imagery coherent across the page. Conventional sections can be appropriate when they explain the actual product; a symbolic organizing idea is optional.

Write section headings that identify their content: pricing, workflow, supported features, or another relevant subject. Use concise factual explanations beneath them. Promotional language can support an explicitly requested brand voice, but custom design does not require slogans, wordplay, or repeated metaphors throughout the page.

Every action needs an honest destination. A static workshop page can lead to service or visit information; it cannot simulate a completed booking. A pricing control must reflect the actual interaction or be clearly identified as a preview. Apply [Forms and journeys](forms-and-journeys.md) only when a real form or consequential flow is in scope.

**Check:** a visitor can identify the offer, material terms, and next action; factual content and imagery remain credible; lower sections continue the argument; links and relevant controls work; representative wide and narrow compositions remain understandable where observed.

**Example:** a bicycle workshop page can organize around diagnosis, agreed work, and collection. Give supplied service prices and opening information clear roles, then lead to how a rider brings in a bicycle. This produces a useful structure from the service itself without invented social proof or a fake booking integration.

## Reading

Identify whether the content explains, references, instructs, or warns. Recover how readers arrive, what they need to find, and what they do after following a link or example. A long article and a searchable reference have different navigation needs even within one site.

Organize headings around meaning and sequence. Use semantic levels for the document structure and visual styles for their reading roles. Establish a comfortable measure and clear relationships between body text, notes, examples, code, tables, and figures. Avoid treating every paragraph as display copy or shrinking complex content until it fits.

For long content, provide useful wayfinding where the task warrants it: a section index, meaningful anchors, breadcrumbs, or a clear return route. Preserve location after an internal link. Check that sticky navigation does not cover the target heading. Keep labels descriptive enough to predict the destination.

Use [Craft](../craft.md#typography) for reading roles and [responsive composition](../craft.md#responsive-composition) for reflow. Code or genuinely two-dimensional material can use a local scrolling region while surrounding prose remains readable. Keep explanations and captions accessible alongside their visual material.

**Check:** readers can locate a needed section, follow links and examples, and recover their place; headings and reading order remain meaningful; actual code, tables, figures, long labels, and enlarged text remain usable where observed. Source structure and rendered readability are different evidence.

**Example:** a knowledge article reached through search should identify its subject and place in the collection, expose useful section headings, and provide a return path. A narrow viewport can adapt the index without covering the article or removing section access.

## Exploration

Identify the actual collection, what distinguishes an item, and how visitors discover, select, understand, and leave it. Recover available titles, descriptions, authorship, dates, tags, media, or other meaningful metadata. Do not fabricate clients or project outcomes to make a portfolio appear complete.

Let the strongest real work establish scale and character. Choose an overview and detail relationship suited to the collection: the visitor may need comparison, browsing, a sequence, or a focused artifact view. Display metadata according to its role in selection rather than applying every available tag to every card.

Implement search, filters, ordering, and query retention only when the task calls for them. Define matching, no-results feedback, and clearing where applicable. Keep item identity stable across overview and detail. Preserve useful browsing context on return using the project's navigation model; persistent storage is not required merely to support a coherent local path.

Make navigation discoverable and operable across keyboard, touch, and pointer input. Do not make essential item information or actions available only on hover. Distinctive composition and motion can express the work, but must not conceal it, monopolize scrolling, or create an unnecessary entry gate. Preserve a useful reduced-motion experience.

**Check:** an item can be found, opened, understood, and left; its relevant context and requested next actions remain available; keyboard and narrow-screen paths are usable where observed; incomplete media and no-result states are treated honestly when relevant.

**Example:** a searchable collection with article detail combines exploration for finding an item and reading for consuming it. Preserve query context on return, give the article its own hierarchy, and check both halves of the path. A decorative grid alone does not complete that request.

## Mixed surfaces and evidence

Identify a dominant purpose and apply secondary guidance locally. A product page can embed an application preview with meaningful records and controls; its promotional composition should not dictate the density of that preview. An article can contain a collection without turning every paragraph into a browsing card.

Use the relevant [Craft](../craft.md) and [Interaction](../interaction.md) sections to execute decisions. Review the complete requested target with [Review](../review.md), selecting evidence that can establish its content, visual, and behavioral requirements. When rendering or interaction is unavailable, retain the same task while reporting those aspects as unverified.
